# How camera assistants choose a shot

The owner reports poor composition in 0.8.0. That build demonstrated automatic subject proposals and a route into another view. The desktop evaluation counted proposed turns and framing guides. It had no human preference results. The next benchmark should measure the choice of photograph directly.

## What the palm-tree clip shows

Framed starts from a 0.5× preview where the upper-right crown is already visible. It suggests aiming up and right, then the viewfinder moves to 1.8×. A local desktop test matched image features from four later views at 7.45–9.30 s to three earlier wide views at 3.10–4.15 s. The estimated overlap was about 100% of the later frame in the wide view across those pairs. A centered half-width, half-height window in the same wide image covered about half the later framing. For the illustrated 3.10 s to 9.30 s pair, the fit had 72 inliers spread over 72% of the later image's width and 89% of its height.

The overlap test decodes the first frame at or after each timestamp, extracts source pixels x 65–654 and y 150–994, masks the outer 10 pixels and status-text region, and matches SIFT features with a 0.71 distance-ratio test. It fits a robust homography at a three-pixel threshold and intersects the projected later viewport with the earlier viewport and the central window. Sources, the analysis script under `.local/`, and the illustrated match under `artifacts/palm-view-coverage.jpg` stay on this computer. This is an approximate image match from a compressed screen recording. The footage reveals scene coverage, while the developer's internal algorithm and the Vivo's optical behavior remain unknown.

## Where our decision falls short

The automatic CameraX path samples three recent views at the current zoom. The detector and scene classification use the last view. The planner picks one region by confidence or prominence, projects that target into registered views, and evaluates framing against a style and image-detail rules. The turn suggestion also follows that one region. A more accurate tracker would guide the same choice. The 0.8 replay already demonstrated a same-category subject switch between neighboring palms. [Current scene analysis](../app/src/main/java/dev/cameracompositor/LocalComposition.kt), [subject and direction policy](../app/src/main/java/dev/cameracompositor/SceneDiscovery.kt), [style scoring](../app/src/main/java/dev/cameracompositor/CompositionStyles.kt), [0.8 evidence](V080_SCENE_DISCOVERY.md).

## Next evaluation

Collect observed views from the widest usable lens or a short sweep on the main camera. Compare several distinct subjects, angles and zooms against the original on the same scene. Test a learned photographic ranker with evidence about subject placement, separation, clutter, incomplete subjects and background structure. Include a keep decision and cases where the starting frame is already good. Use model-anonymous judgments on held-out multi-view scenes before connecting a replacement planner to the automatic shutter. Keep the phone test focused on guidance, reacquisition, capture and responsiveness once the selection test passes.

PhotoGuider is a relevant desktop reference. It ranks directions from a panorama before refining a shot, and its learned scoring layer uses photographic preferences. The released pipeline needs GPU feature extractors even though the scoring layer is small. Its code license and model/data terms are separate. SPAS predicts a camera turn from rated compositions and reported a 64% preference for adjusted views in its test. These are useful methods and evaluation examples. A local mobile winner for our scenes remains to be established. [PhotoGuider paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02897v1), [inference release](https://github.com/NTUneillee/PhotoGuider), [SPAS study](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03638v1#S7.SS3).

## Vivo V30 teacher demo

The owner narrowed the target to one standard Vivo V30 and chose to use the existing reference clips. The live goal is one repeatable scene in which the app uses a wide observation or a short turn, then guides the photographer to a stronger frame and saves a photo. Verify CameraX lens access and rehearse the capture on that handset before calling the demo ready.

An isolated-view benchmark tested 12 sampled human-rated frames per photo on the existing 20 development and 60 evaluation photos. The same model received the original image and candidate coordinates in one test, and each candidate individually in the other. Of 2,353 held-out pairs separated by at least 0.5 rating points, GAIC agreed with the higher human rating in 96.4% of pairs with context and 74.0% in isolation. The largest-crop baseline agreed in 71.1% of pairs. NIMA in isolation agreed in 61.7%.

The common input context is a strong signal for this model. A live sweep still needs a decision tested with distinct angles. In a first palm-clip replay, GAIC preferred a later wide view near 6.05 seconds when whole video frames were scored. Its most favored variants remained small trims, even when the search included deep zooms. These results support testing a context-aware shortlist of visibly different shots before connecting a model to the shutter. The labeled test uses static crops and the same dataset family as GAIC's training. Its percentages do not fill the live human preference gate.
